Binocular color asymmetry

An IEEE VR 2026 poster investigating per-eye chromatic sensitivity, asymmetric color reduction, and the trade-off between perceptual quality and estimated display power in XR.

Research question

Can stronger chromatic reduction in the non-dominant eye remain perceptually acceptable while lowering display power?

Research approach

The poster combines a four-alternative forced-choice (4AFC) chromatic detection task with per-eye color maps modeled using a radial basis function neural network (RBFNN). The maps guide gaze-contingent chromatic reduction. Paired video judgments (2AFC) are converted to Just-Objectionable-Difference (JOD) scores to assess perceptual acceptability.

Procedure overview

Measure per-eye sensitivity
Use a chromatic detection task to estimate perceptual thresholds separately for the dominant and non-dominant eyes.
Build chromatic reduction maps
Model the thresholds with RBFNNs and use the maps for perceptually informed color reduction.
Validate quality and estimate power
Compare video presentations with paired judgments and JOD scaling, and estimate display power using a calibrated OLED model.

Poster findings

Within the tested conditions, the poster reports broader chromatic tolerance in the non-dominant eye for selected color directions and greater estimated display-power savings with non-dominant-eye-targeted reduction. The maximum reported saving was 24% for S-cone-dominant video content.

The power results are estimates from a calibrated OLED display model; they are not direct measurements of HMD battery-life improvement.
